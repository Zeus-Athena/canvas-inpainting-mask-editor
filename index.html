How AI Masking Works

Inpainting models require two inputs: the original RGB image and a single-channel binary mask image.

  • Black (#000000): Unmodified background pixels to be preserved.
  • White (#FFFFFF): Masked defect regions to be regenerated by generative diffusion or adversarial models.
  • Exact Dimension Match: The exported mask resolution must match the source image at 1:1 pixel parity.

Compatible Inpainting Backends

The exported PNG mask directly plugs into standard computer vision pipelines:

  • LaMa (Large Mask Inpainting): Fast resolution-robust Fourier convolution inpainting.
  • Stable Diffusion & Flux Fill: Latent diffusion inpainting pipelines (diffusers.StableDiffusionInpaintPipeline).
  • OpenCV Inpaint: Fast local boundary inpainting via cv2.INPAINT_TELEA and cv2.INPAINT_NS.

How does the mask thresholding algorithm work?
User strokes are drawn onto an overlay canvas using semi-transparent rgba strokes for visual feedback. Upon export, an offscreen canvas extracts the alpha channel of every pixel: pixels with alpha > 20 are mapped to #FFFFFF (255, 255, 255), and transparent areas are mapped to #000000 (0, 0, 0).
